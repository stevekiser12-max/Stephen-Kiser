# FHB Total-Person Employee Assessment

**Modeled on the PXT Select framework — Favored Home Buyers**
Version 1.0 · For acquisitions and dispo hiring · Finalist stage only

---

## 0. How This Test Works (Read First)

This assessment mirrors the three-domain logic of PXT Select. It does **not** grade
pass/fail. It produces scores that are compared against a **Performance Model** — the
score ranges that describe a strong FHB rep in a specific seat. A candidate either
falls **inside** a range (fit) or **outside** it (gap → probe in the interview).

Three domains, weighted exactly like the source framework:

| Domain | What it predicts | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Thinking Style** (cognitive) | Ramp speed inside the 90-day gate; trainability | **40%** |
| **B. Behavioral Traits** (9 scales) | How the person works; fit to the seat | **40%** |
| **C. Interests** (ranked) | Whether they'll *like* the work → retention | **20%** |

**Administration**
- Untimed but expect ~50–60 min. Online, unsupervised, any device.
- Calculator allowed on Section A only. No other aids.
- Same test, same scoring, for every candidate in a role. One input among several —
  never a sole knockout. Target: ~1/3 of the hiring decision.

**Two seats covered:** Acquisitions (Acq) and Dispo. The Performance Models differ;
the questions do not.

## Section A — Thinking Style (Cognitive)

*40% of fit. Measures learning speed and problem-solving. Calculator allowed.*
*24 items, 4 sub-scales. In a live/adaptive build this section would serve items by
difficulty; here it is a fixed form so it can be printed and hand-scored.*

### A1. Verbal Skill (vocabulary) — 6 items

Choose the word closest in meaning.

1. **Concede** — (a) refuse (b) admit/yield (c) delay (d) argue
2. **Prudent** — (a) reckless (b) wealthy (c) careful (d) rude
3. **Mitigate** — (a) worsen (b) lessen (c) confuse (d) repeat
4. **Candid** — (a) sweet (b) hidden (c) frank (d) nervous
5. **Tentative** — (a) certain (b) provisional (c) permanent (d) hostile
6. **Discrepancy** — (a) agreement (b) discount (c) inconsistency (d) delay

*Answers: 1‑b, 2‑c, 3‑b, 4‑c, 5‑b, 6‑c*

### A2. Verbal Reasoning (draw conclusions from text) — 6 items

7. *A seller will only accept an all-cash offer that closes in 14 days. Our buyer needs
   21 days.* Which is best supported?
   (a) The deal is dead. (b) The buyer must be all-cash. (c) The timeline, not the price,
   is the current obstacle. (d) The seller wants more money.
   → **(c)**

8. *Every lead from Channel X this month was a wrong number or a renter. Channel Y
   produced 3 contracts.* A reasonable conclusion:
   (a) Channel X should get more budget. (b) Channel Y converts and X is not producing
   sellers. (c) Renters never sell. (d) Channel Y is cheaper.
   → **(b)**

9. *"We can assign this contract only if the purchase agreement has no
   anti-assignment clause."* The contract has such a clause. Therefore:
   (a) We can still assign it. (b) We cannot assign it as written. (c) The clause is
   unenforceable. (d) We must close ourselves — always.
   → **(b)**

10. *All of our best months followed weeks where reps made 250+ dials. Last week reps
    made 90 dials.* Most defensible statement:
    (a) This month will be bad. (b) Dials guarantee deals. (c) Activity was below the
    level historically tied to strong months. (d) Reps are lazy.
    → **(c)**

11. *A homeowner says "I'm not interested" but keeps asking what the house is worth.*
    Best inference:
    (a) Hang up. (b) There may be latent interest worth exploring. (c) They want a free
    appraisal only. (d) They're a competitor.
    → **(b)**

12. *If ARV is $300k and we need 30% margin plus $40k rehab, then a $170k offer…*
    (a) exceeds our max. (b) is within a defensible range. (c) cannot be evaluated.
    (d) ignores rehab.
    → **(b)**  *(300k − 40k = 260k; 70% of 300k = 210k; 170k sits below both → safe.)*

*Answers: 7‑c, 8‑b, 9‑b, 10‑c, 11‑b, 12‑b*

### A3. Numerical Ability (raw calculation) — 6 items

13. 15% of 240 = ? → **36**
14. $1,850 + $2,995 + $410 = ? → **$5,255**
15. 3/4 of 88 = ? → **66**
16. $18,000 ÷ 12 = ? → **$1,500**
17. 240,000 × 0.70 = ? → **168,000**
18. If a number increased from 80 to 100, the % increase is? → **25%**

### A4. Numeric Reasoning (applied word problems) — 6 items

19. ARV $250,000. Max Allowable Offer = 70% of ARV − $35,000 rehab. **MAO = ?**
    → 0.70 × 250,000 = 175,000; − 35,000 = **$140,000**

20. You assign a contract for a $12,000 fee. Marketing cost to source that deal was
    $3,500. **Net = ?** → **$8,500**

21. A rep closes 2 deals per 60 contacts. To close 5 deals, how many contacts (same
    rate)? → 60/2 = 30 per deal × 5 = **150 contacts**

22. Seller owes $90,000 on the mortgage and wants $15,000 in cash. Your all-in acquisition
    number to satisfy them is at least? → **$105,000**

23. Sequence: 5, 10, 20, 40, __ → **80**

24. A deal nets $10,000, split 50/50 with a JV partner, then you pay a 10% referral on
    *your* half. **Your take-home = ?** → 10,000/2 = 5,000; − 10% = **$4,500**

**Section A scoring:** 1 point per correct item, 24 max. Convert to a 1–10 index using
the band table in §D. Report **Learning Index** = the composite of all four sub-scales.

## Section B — Behavioral Traits (9 Scales)

*40% of fit. Self-report. There are no right answers — each scale is a continuum and
neither end is "good" or "bad." Scored 1–10 (STEN-style) relative to a working-adult
norm, not % correct.*

**Instructions to candidate:** Rate how well each statement describes you.
`1 = Strongly Disagree · 2 = Disagree · 3 = Neutral · 4 = Agree · 5 = Strongly Agree.`
Answer honestly and quickly — a consistency check runs on this section (see §E).

Each of the 9 traits is measured by 4 items (36 total). Items marked **(R)** are
reverse-scored. Scale raw score = sum of its 4 items (reverse items = 6 − response),
range 4–20, then mapped to 1–10 in §D.

### Pace — *Steady (low) ↔ Urgent (high)*
1. I feel restless when work moves slowly.
2. I'd rather act now and adjust than wait for the perfect moment.
3. I prefer a calm, predictable pace. **(R)**
4. Deadlines energize me more than they stress me.

### Assertiveness — *Unassuming (low) ↔ Forceful (high)*
5. I push my point of view even when others resist.
6. I take charge of a room without being asked.
7. I'd rather let others lead the conversation. **(R)**
8. I'm comfortable telling someone their offer won't work.

### Sociability — *Reserved (low) ↔ Outgoing (high)*
9. Talking to strangers all day energizes me.
10. I build rapport with new people quickly.
11. I need quiet, solo time to recharge. **(R)**
12. I enjoy cold outreach.

### Conformity — *Strong-willed (low) ↔ Compliant (high)*
13. I follow the script/process even when I'd do it differently.
14. I check with a manager before deviating from policy.
15. I trust my own judgment over the established playbook. **(R)**
16. I'm comfortable operating inside tight rules.

### Outlook — *Skeptical (low) ↔ Trusting (high)*
17. I assume people are telling me the truth until proven otherwise.
18. I take what sellers tell me at face value.
19. I look for the catch in most deals. **(R)**
20. I give people the benefit of the doubt.

### Decisiveness — *Deliberate (low) ↔ Bold (high)*
21. I make calls fast, even with incomplete information.
22. I'm comfortable committing to a number on the first call.
23. I like to gather all the facts before deciding. **(R)**
24. I trust my gut on close calls.

### Accommodation — *Steadfast (low) ↔ Agreeable (high)*
25. I'd rather find middle ground than hold my line.
26. I avoid conflict when I can.
27. I hold firm on my position under pressure. **(R)**
28. Keeping the other side happy matters to me in a negotiation.

### Independence — *Reliant (low) ↔ Autonomous (high)*
29. I do my best work with little supervision.
30. I set my own daily priorities without being told.
31. I prefer clear direction on exactly what to do. **(R)**
32. I hold myself accountable to my own numbers.

### Judgment — *Intuitive (low) ↔ Factual (high)*
33. I base decisions on data more than instinct.
34. I want the comps and numbers before I commit.
35. I often "just know" the right move without the analysis. **(R)**
36. I document my reasoning with facts.

## Section C — Interests (Forced-Choice Ranking)

*20% of fit. Predicts motivation and retention. No right answer.*

**Rank all six from 1 (most appealing) to 6 (least). No ties.**

- **Enterprising** — persuading, negotiating, closing deals, driving revenue.
- **People Service** — helping people through a stressful situation, guiding them.
- **Financial/Admin** — numbers, contracts, tracking, organizing pipelines.
- **Technical** — systems, tools, CRMs, data, analysis.
- **Mechanical** — hands-on, building/fixing physical things.
- **Creative** — design, writing, novel ideas, marketing angles.

Record the candidate's **top 3** in order. Compare to the role's top 3 in §D.

### D3. The Performance Models

Ideal is a **range**, not a point. Build the real thing by benchmarking your own top
performers (Jo/Cliff/Adam for Acq; Ivan/Matt for Dispo) — the ranges below are a
starting template, not gospel.

| Scale | **Acquisitions** target | **Dispo** target |
|---|---|---|
| Learning Index (cog) | 6–10 | 6–10 |
| Pace | 6–9 (urgent) | 5–8 |
| Assertiveness | 6–9 (forceful) | 6–9 |
| Sociability | 6–10 (outgoing) | 5–8 |
| Conformity | 4–7 (follows core process, some autonomy) | 4–7 |
| Outlook | 3–6 (healthy skepticism) | 4–7 |
| Decisiveness | 6–9 (bold) | 5–8 |
| Accommodation | 3–6 (holds the line) | 5–8 (deal-greaser) |
| Independence | 6–10 (self-driven) | 6–9 |
| Judgment | 4–7 (balances gut + comps) | 6–9 (data-led on pricing) |
| **Interests top 3** | Enterprising, People Service, Financial/Admin | Enterprising, Technical/Financial, People Service |

### D4. Compute Overall Fit %

1. **Section A fit** — 100% if Learning Index is inside the range, else scale down by
   distance (e.g., one band below the floor = ~70%).
2. **Section B fit** — for each of the 9 scales, score 1 if inside range, partial if
   one STEN outside, 0 if far outside. Average the 9. Convert to %.
3. **Section C fit** — count how many of the role's top-3 interests appear in the
   candidate's top-3 (2/3 = ~67%, 3/3 = 100%). Order matters as a tiebreak.
4. **Overall Fit = (A × 0.40) + (B × 0.40) + (C × 0.20).**

### D5. Decision Rules (not vibes)

| Overall Fit vs. custom model | Action |
|---|---|
| **≥ 80%** | Advance |
| **65–79%** | Advance **only if** the interview clears the flagged gaps |
| **< 65%**, or distortion flag + weak interview | Out |

Rule of thumb from the source: ~70%+ is a generally acceptable fit. Keep this as
**~1/3** of the decision, next to interview and track record.

## Section E — Distortion (Consistency) Check

The behavioral section includes reverse-scored pairs that should move in opposite
directions. Flag possible **impression management** if either holds:

- **Reverse-pair inconsistency:** on 3+ traits, the reverse item and its same-direction
  items both scored high (e.g., agreeing with both "I take charge" and "I'd rather let
  others lead").
- **Extreme-desirability pattern:** 30+ of 36 answers are 4–5 with almost no 1–2s, and
  every trait lands in the role's ideal zone (too perfect to be real).

A flag is a **probability, not proof.** Guidance: don't auto-reject — lean harder on the
interview and re-probe the "perfect" traits.

## Section F — Auto-Targeted Interview Guide

For every scale where the candidate falls **outside** the model range, ask the paired
question and note what to listen for. (This is the PXT "question per gap" mechanic.)

| Gap | Ask | Listen for |
|---|---|---|
| Cognitive below range | "Walk me through how you'd calculate a max offer on a $220k ARV with $30k rehab." | Can they reason through it live, not just recall a formula. |
| Pace too low (Acq) | "Tell me about a time you had to move on something before you felt fully ready." | Real urgency vs. discomfort with speed. |
| Assertiveness too low | "Describe pushing back on a seller/agent who wanted terms you couldn't give." | Do they hold a position or fold. |
| Sociability too low (Acq) | "How do you feel after a full day of cold calls?" | Drained-and-done vs. energized. |
| Conformity too high | "When did you deviate from a script and why?" | Judgment, not blind rule-following. |
| Outlook too high (Acq) | "A seller's story doesn't match the comps. What do you do?" | Verifies vs. takes at face value. |
| Decisiveness too low | "Last time you had to commit to a number with incomplete info?" | Comfort deciding under ambiguity. |
| Accommodation too high (Acq) | "Tell me about a negotiation where you had to hold firm." | Can they resist just splitting the difference. |
| Independence too low | "Describe a week where nobody told you what to do. What happened?" | Self-direction and self-accountability. |
| Judgment gap (Dispo) | "How do you price a wholesale deal for your buyers list?" | Data-led, comps-driven pricing. |
| Interest mismatch | "What part of this job would you most look forward to on a Monday?" | Alignment with Enterprising/People Service. |
| Distortion flagged | Re-ask 2–3 "perfect" traits behaviorally. | Consistency between self-report and stories. |

## Section G — Deployment Notes for FHB

1. **Benchmark before you trust it.** Have current top performers take the full test;
   replace the template ranges in D3 with their actual score patterns. A library "sales
   rep" model ≠ a virtual wholesaling acquisitions rep.
2. **Finalists only.** This is a scored, cost-bearing step — slot it post-screen,
   pre-final-interview. Hand Section F to whoever runs finals.
3. **Cross-check DISC (Alfred).** The 9 traits map loosely onto DISC — compare
   classifications; disagreement = probe.
4. **Feed Zoho.** Store Overall Fit %, Learning Index, and the 9 trait STENs as candidate
   fields. After 6 months, correlate against close rate / ramp. If fit % doesn't predict
   production, rebuild the model.
5. **Reuse for team ops.** Plot the pod on the 9 behavioral scales for composition gaps;
   run a manager-vs-report comparison for anyone managing a new rep.
6. **Legal hygiene.** Same test, same scoring, every candidate in a role. One input among
   several. Never a sole knockout.
